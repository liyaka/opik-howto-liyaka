# Swarm Prevention — Larkspur Hollow Apiary Handbook

## Swarm season
At Larkspur Hollow the swarm season runs from the second week of May to the end of June. During these weeks inspect every seven days, not nine, because a colony can build a queen cell and leave within eight days.

## Warning signs
Queen cups holding an egg or a larva mean the colony is preparing to swarm. A cluster of bees hanging at the hive entrance, called bearding, together with a crowded brood nest are the other two signs. Finding two or more charged queen cells is the trigger for action the same day.

## The split method
When queen cells are found, move the old queen and three frames of brood into a new box at least three metres from the original hive. The original hive keeps one queen cell and raises a new queen. Mark the date on the lid so the new queen is checked for laying after 28 days.

## Giving space
Add a super when seven of the ten frames in the top box are covered with bees. A crowded colony swarms; a colony with room to store nectar usually does not.
